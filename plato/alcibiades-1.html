About This Text

Dramatic date c. 432 BC. Alcibiades is about twenty, about to enter public life, before the campaign at Potidaea.

Socrates meets Alcibiades and says he has not spoken to him all these years because a divine sign held him back, and the sign has only now let him approach. Alcibiades is about to address the Assembly and is sure he can advise Athens on war and peace. Socrates asks what he would be advising about, and what he knows that the Assembly does not. Alcibiades says he knows what is just, then admits he never learned it and never found he lacked it. Socrates leads him to say that the just and the advantageous are the same, and that he has been answering from his own confusion. The talk turns to what it is to care for oneself rather than for one's belongings. The user of a thing is different from the thing he uses, so the man is the soul that uses the body. To know oneself is to look into another soul, as an eye sees itself in the pupil of an eye. Alcibiades agrees that he must be educated before he rules, and that if the city corrupts him it will be the stronger of the two.

Love in this dialogue

Socrates finally speaks to the proud young Alcibiades, the lover who stayed after all the others left. He shows him he isn't ready to lead Athens because he doesn't know himself. To love someone is to love their soul and help it see itself.
