Plato · Tetralogy V

Χαρμίδης

Charmides

153a–176d · Stephanus

John Burnet, Platonis Opera (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1913), vol. III

The story

Socrates comes back from the camp at Potidaea and goes straight to Taureas' palaestra. Chaerephon greets him, and Critias tells him about the beautiful Charmides, who is said to be temperate as well. Charmides enters and the room turns to look. Socrates is asked to pretend he has a cure for the boy's headache. Looking inside Charmides' cloak, he is thrown off balance, then recovers and says the charm only works if the soul is treated first. He questions Charmides on what temperance is. The boy tries quietness, then modesty, then doing one's own work, and Critias takes over the last of these. Critias offers self-knowledge, and then a knowledge that knows itself and every other knowledge. Socrates shows that such a knowledge would not, by itself, give us the knowledge of good and bad that actually benefits us. They reach no definition. Critias tells Charmides to attach himself to Socrates, and Charmides says he will use force if he has to. Socrates says there is no resisting the two of them.

Love in this dialogue

Back from battle, Socrates meets the stunningly beautiful Charmides and catches a glimpse inside his cloak, which almost unsettles him. He steadies himself, and they try and fail to define self-control. Self-mastery is what lets love see instead of grab.
